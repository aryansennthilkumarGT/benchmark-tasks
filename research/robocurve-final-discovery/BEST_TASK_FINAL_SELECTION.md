# First investment: Toyota YUBI return-spring installation

**Choose YUBI,84/100, for the next bounded CPU contact coupon only.** Research selection is complete; a complete task, native demonstration and review qualification are not. Faze4 is a close83/100 rigid backup. Neither has passed full stock-YAM assembly access.

Toyota publishes an actual teleoperation glove, matching registered STEP, an explicit installation photograph/procedure and a named No16 band. Installing that band equips robot teleoperation hardware with a real finger spring. The task has few essential interfaces—two hooks, a groove and the moving finger—rather than a full tendon transmission, twelve tiny springs or a coupled rolling reducer. This supports the cheapest finite first mechanism investment among the source-rich robot candidates.

The strongest elastic comparator is active[#1872](https://github.com/robocurve/benchmark-tasks/pull/1872), with closed[#1519](https://github.com/robocurve/benchmark-tasks/pull/1519) also relevant. Their actual definitions/code grasp rigid hooks to restrain cargo with an elastic span. YUBI grasps/deforms a closed soft loop onto fixed hooks and a groove to create a moving-joint spring. This is a defensible central-ability difference; different hardware alone would not be enough. No exhaustive ownership clearance or official acceptance is asserted.

## The executed result changes the investment boundary

The [CPU study](cpu_preflight/README.md) used the existing robot reference,315 hulls per arm, both arms,45 original source-body envelopes and28 source-local placements at a real hook. Every envelope case was inconclusive. Narrow phase on the least-overlapping case measured26.395mm³ of actual right-fingertip/FLAP_R intersection; another pair separated by1.485mm and a third touched. That **particular direct-hand pose is rejected**. It is not permissible to delete FLAP_R, which the manual installs before the band.

This lowered the initial88 score to84. No empty-hand pose passed, no rubber band was simulated, and no stable band purchase, material response, continuous route or final return function was demonstrated. The screen also omitted fixture geometry and arm self-collision certification; it cannot certify the full task. The next coupon must begin with a different source-compatible approach or an ordinary held tool, including actual acquisition/withdrawal. The holder-hook saddle still needs unambiguous interpretation from the already available CAD.

## Why it wins narrowly

- **Faze4:** stronger rigid geometry, but registration of print-oriented parts, dual orbital closure, rolling-loop stability, cover and tiny fastener handling add cost. A single-disk ratio exploit also needs independent inventory inspection.
- **Toshiba:** fewer material-model dependencies and a recognizable household procedure, but no matched interface/cavity dimensions, uncertain broad-glass purchase and meaningful overlap with keyed kitchen nesting.
- **Chefman/Oster:** genuine documented contacts, but original-PDF mirrors and unmeasured small interfaces/compliance leave more reconstruction work and weaker importance than a direct robot build.
- **CELLO:** genuine tractable brake geometry; however, actual link registration/mass/CoM and a loose-brake sag baseline must establish that a nontrivial hold/backdrive window exists.
- **Other finalists:** the lower ranks are alternatives, not simultaneous implementation commitments. They incur small-part handling, source-interface, full-equipment access, thin-line, licensing or contested-novelty costs documented in their dossiers.
