Fit a keyed pulley on a shaft
Task board:
TK-ZSRSAK
Media:
M-NE959U (the setup screenshot, the Dream-1 run and the scripted demo are on the task board under this ID)
Category:
Impact on jobs
Run limit:
2,400 steps (2 minutes at 20 Hz)
Task:
Put the yellow key in the shaft's keyway, then slide the aluminium pulley onto the shaft over the key, up against the shoulder, and let go of everything.
Description:
Fitting a keyed pulley, sprocket or coupling hub on a shaft is routine machinery maintenance for general maintenance and repair workers (O*NET 49-9071), industrial machinery mechanics (49-9041), millwrights (49-9044) and HVAC mechanics (49-9021). The parts are true scale: a DIN 6885 parallel key (8 x 7 x 40 mm, 17 g) in 8.2 mm keyways (0.1 mm a side), a 25 mm steel shaft in a 25.5 mm bore (0.25 mm a side), an 80 mm aluminium V-belt pulley (217 g). The shaft turns freely in a pillow-block bearing and a hand crank on its end hangs down, so at rest its keyway faces the robot: one hand has to turn the crank and hold the keyway up while the other lays the key in, lines the pulley's keyway up with it and slides the pulley home against the shoulder.
What is new against the board:
the skill is a two-part keyed fit on a shaft that is free to turn. The shaft is a pendulum: let go, its keyway swings round to face the robot and a key laid in it falls out. So one hand has to find the crank, turn the shaft a quarter turn and hold it there against gravity while the other hand places a separate loose part (the key) into an open keyway, and then turns a second part (the pulley) about its own axis until its keyway lines up with that key (0.1 mm a side) before sliding it home on a 0.25 mm fit. Nearby accepted tasks test single-part fits on fixed shafts or housings:
#1800
slides one sleeve into a fixed housing and turns it to register a port, with nothing turning under it and no second part to place;
#1113
feeds a hex shaft through two bores, where the hex itself sets the angle and no key is involved. Pending
#1864
(another team, being revised) presses a horn straight onto a fixed splined servo shaft with one hand: the spline is part of the shaft. Here the angle is set by a separate key the robot places itself, and the shaft must be counter-held throughout, as a mechanic does when keying a pulley, sprocket or coupling hub.
Scores:
Dream-1 run: 0.0 (both arms reached out over the shaft stand and stayed there, the left one nudging the crank once; neither picked up the key or the pulley); demo: 1.0 (all four steps and the parts still from step 1125, held 1 s: 1.0 at step 1145)
Setup
Dream-1 run (GIF at 2x speed)
Scripted demo (GIF at 2x speed)
Scoring: four steps, 0.2 each
Step
Done when
1. Key in the shaft's keyway
on the keyway's floor (within 0.5 mm), within 0.5 mm of its middle line, lined up with the shaft, ends inside the keyway; not held
2. Pulley started
with step 1, the pulley's bore around the shaft (axes within 1 mm and 3 degrees), at least 10 mm on
3. Pulley home
with step 2, its face within 2 mm of the shoulder and the key inside the pulley's keyway too
4. Released
with step 3, no gripper holds the pulley and both are at least 3 cm from the key, the pulley and the crank handle
Score
When
1.0 (ends the run)
every full-success condition has held together, without a break, for 1 s: all four steps (so both hands have let go and are clear), nothing off the table, and the key, the pulley and the shaft each within 2 mm and 2 degrees of where they were when that second began
0.2 per step, at most 0.8
otherwise, including while that second is being timed
0
the key or the pulley off the table, or a state that is not finite
The 1 s is timed in the task file itself: every control step,
_Progress
notes when the full-success conditions first held together and the parts' poses at that moment, and any lapse (a step undone, a gripper back within 3 cm, the key, the pulley or the shaft moved more than 2 mm or turned more than 2 degrees) clears it, so the second starts over.
success()
and
score()
return 1.0 only once that second is complete;
SUCCESS_HOLD_S
is 0, so the run ends on that same step. The tests check no 1.0 after 0.95 s and 1.0 at 1 s, that a lapse restarts the second, and that 1.0 is never reached over 3 s while any one condition is missing (9 cases). A pulley pushed home without the key, or with its keyway off the key, scores nothing for steps 2 and 3.
Top camera, 20 Hz control, 240 Hz physics. Run commands, the demo's timeline, assets and the real-station build are in the task's README.
