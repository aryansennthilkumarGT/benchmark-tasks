## ODRI: exact source sections; reduced contact model

Designer [Foot Contact Switch v1](https://github.com/open-dynamic-robot-initiative/open_robot_actuator_hardware/tree/66af1522b4fba0ec4a1d7790e66f5e4652208d30/mechanics/foot_contact_switch_v1), pin `66af1522b4fba0ec4a1d7790e66f5e4652208d30`. The pinned-source README, drawing, photographs, three STEP parts and SolidWorks archive were inspected/reused. All inputs required by this coupon are now curated in `sources/odri/` with origin hashes in `sources/ODRI_INPUTS.json`; they do not depend on untracked files in an earlier source checkout. Copyright Max Planck Gesellschaft and New York University; source states BSD 3-Clause. The hash manifest identifies exact inputs.

Known from designer: twelve silicone segments, 6 mm OD / 4 mm ID / 3 mm length; six per side; moving foot/sensor/silicone assembly approximately 10 g; complete lower leg 35 g; up to 2 mm foot deflection; antirotation pin 1.5×12 mm; two 10 mm electronics boards. The component photograph attributes 7 g to foot/tread/pin, 2 g to electronics and 1 g to silicone. The model therefore uses a 9 g moving foot body plus 1 g distributed over twelve rings. These are approximate designer values, not our weighed measurements.

The actual STEP optical axis is X. The foot's native pin direction +Y is rotated +90° about X to point along leg +Z; its center is translated to source `(0,0,-160)` mm. Exact STEP Boolean checks give zero foot/base and foot/fork overlap at this registration. This supports the selected local registration; it does not validate all electronics, wiring, pin seating or fasteners. STEP and print-STL frames differ. The initial incorrect STL-frame plot is retained only under `debug/` and is excluded from evidence.

CAD sections at X=±2.123 mm pass through the two real pad banks (the foot membrane has faces at ±0.75 mm). Using one mid-bank section over the 3 mm tube breadth omits axial chamfers/end-contact variation; it is not a full 3D tube fit. Tessellation uses 0.015 mm linear deflection; numerical signed-distance spacing is 0.05 mm. Sections preserve holes by nested-loop XOR; invalid contours are rejected rather than filled. Additional valid sections and the independent SDF error check are retained. No cavity was enlarged to fit the silicone.

The guide channel has actual R0.85 mm cylindrical ends at Y=±0.3 mm, Z=16–18 mm above the foot center. With the specified R0.75 mm pin, nominal lateral center clearance is ±0.4 mm. The model includes unilateral pin/slot normal contact beyond that clearance; this is not a joint hold/limit. The foot pin bore spans native Y=9.9–13.5 mm. A 12 mm pin remains engaged with the 2 mm guide for any seating depth between 0 and 3.6 mm throughout the ±1.4 mm numerical vertical domain. Actual pin seating depth and real printed tolerances were not measured. Idealized zero rotation is a reduction, not a calibrated representation of pin/foot rotational compliance. The preliminary pin-omitted runs are excluded.

Each tube is a free closed 32-node curved beam: mean radius 2.5 mm, wall 1 mm, axial breadth 3 mm. Stretch stiffness derives from `EA/l`; bending from `EI/l`, with rectangular wall `I=b*t^3/12`. It can ovalize, translate, lose contact and self-contact. This geometrically nonlinear beam is an approximation to the actual hollow silicone section, not a solid-material/FEM validation. Neither rings nor foot are anchored to a neutral target. Initial circular rings are an overlapping optimization guess inside the narrow source gap, not a physically admissible installation state or insertion trajectory. The first equilibrium solves the precompressed state; optimizer iterations are not real-time motion. Subsequent force removal tests a nearby free equilibrium, not a measured dynamic return or dwell.

**Unmeasured:** silicone compound/hardness, Young's modulus, finite-strain constitutive response, friction, hysteresis, compression set, print tolerances and damage. Nominal E=1 MPa and tested 0.5–2 MPa are authored uncertainty values, not manufacturer specifications. Contact penalty 20 N/mm and cubic signed-distance interpolation are numerical regularizations. Contacts are frictionless in this coupon. The published force-versus-voltage plot is not a force-versus-displacement curve and cannot identify E; no material or optical calibration is claimed.

The model is quasistatic and planar, with idealized zero foot rotation and no axial escape. It does not resolve small rotation/clearance coupling, torsion, finite-time dynamic return, actual fork tightening, wiring or electrical output. Force probes are declared external inspection loads; the foot displacement is solved, not commanded. Numerical boundaries only restrict the validity region and reject escaped cases.

YAM uses the unchanged source pin/hulls and the previous CPU infrastructure. The full-scale leg/foot CAD remains present; the uninstalled fork is parked separately. Tool dimensions and fixture placement are authored ordinary tweezer/support envelopes. PCB axial positions/thickness and pin placement remain conservative uncertain envelopes. Intentional support contact exempts only right fingertips against the foot rim; palms, wrists, leg and tool contacts remain active. No held-tool/support relationship is a physical grasp proof.
