## CRITICAL ROBOT FEASIBILITY STANDARD

An acceptable access result must demonstrate:

- Real source-scale object geometry.
- Original stock YAM collision bodies.
- Both arms and their interaction.
- Realistic gripper or ordinary tool geometry.
- A support strategy that does not already collide.
- Required acquisition and release.
- A credible continuous path or a conservative swept-path certificate.
- No deletion of essential task obstacles.
- No fictitious grasp or externally prescribed carried-object attachment.

Numerical IK success is not grasp success.

Collision-free endpoints are not a swept-path certificate.

A tool that is attached to the gripper by assumption has not demonstrated physical purchase.

Do not fabricate a passing result.

## PHYSICAL SCORING STANDARD

The chosen task must produce an externally inspectable, persistent physical outcome.

Execute meaningful wrong cases before calling a mechanism successful.

Distinguish:

- Physical failures.
- Numerical solver failures.
- Incomplete but physically valid states.
- Invalid or damaging states.

A negative-control run that fails numerically cannot be called a validated physical failure.
