## 1. N013 — assemble a genuine cable gland

**Why this leads:** recognizable electrician installation; compact real hardware; useful released strain-relief/seal endpoint; practical physical tug/mark inspection; plausible stock-gripper/tool access. It is a documented product class, not a generic spring fixture with an occupational label.

**Actual comparison evidence:** PR17's task.json fixes the gland and welds the cable beside it; it tests drip-loop routing, not gland assembly. The actual wire-splice definition at1411 joins conductors with a twist-on spring; accepted1562 makes a pipe olive joint. These are useful close comparisons, not evidence that all gland-assembly submissions are absent.

**First work before implementation:** retrieve the exact LAPP gland manual/cable range and O*NET duty; select a real complete SKU; document its body/locknut/seal/nut sequence and torque; inspect actual clamp/seal geometry and gripper/tool access; resolve novelty versus compression fittings; specify a physically meaningful noninvasive retention test. Stop on an earlier same-ability task or incompatible force/access, rather than enlarging controls.

**Compared with prior failures:** exact commercial interfaces and observable physical retention replace a generic apparatus; no invented multicell fixture; no electrical/server-service label borrowed after deleting connectors. A generic tightened nut with an absent seal still cannot pass.

**Cost outlook:** compact rigid geometry plus a limited seal/contact model may be cheaper than whole-cloth or multi-metre flexible tasks, but thread/torque and retention calibration remain real risks. No measured budget/runtime exists.

## 2. N085 — load a genuine trimmer head with line

**Why this is second:** real landscaping consumable replenishment with an identifiable OEM mechanism and a useful feed-ready endpoint. It moves beyond our old cargo/collet/chuck concepts and beyond another bolt or connector installation.

**Actual comparison evidence:** the examined overhand-knot, tape-wrapping and cable-carrier tasks share flexible-material primitives but have different object roles and end conditions. None of those sources proves equivalent intake winding and functioning line feed. The direct GitHub keyword query was rate-limited, so no exhaustive novelty claim is made.

**First work before implementation:** retrieve ECHO's exact Speed-Feed manual and line specification; confirm ordinary loading without removing essential tool geometry; establish eyelet/knob/feed mechanism and torque; prove stock fingers can thread and wind genuine line; compare other landscaping/head-loading tasks; specify external leader and feed checks. Stop if simulation can only prepose line into a spool or if another earlier same-kind operation matches.

**Compared with prior failures:** real published service mechanism and normal consumable task replace an invented maintenance fixture; a functioning OEM feed check replaces hidden-coordinate-only success. Landscape employment alone still does not prove regular core loading; source that link.

**Cost outlook:** threading and winding are likely much harder than rigid-part count suggests. Full line topology/contact can dominate debugging. Use a bounded contact/grasp feasibility study after source/novelty clearance; switch to N005 or N077 if the real line mechanism is not affordable to preserve.
