## Physical score and earliest cheap disqualifiers

A legitimate future score would inspect actual helix enclosure of the intended wires over source-bound endpoints, after both hands release. It must distinguish wires outside the helix, incomplete winding, a loose tool-held protector, wrong/insufficient span and incorrect anchors/slack. Normal open-helix gaps are not automatically defects: maximum allowed opening/coverage must come from the selected product/application, not an invented turn count. End photographs plus a ruler and relevant views could measure coverage; repeatable retained-state inspection needs a justified benign check. A visible wrap alone does not certify abrasion lifetime, internal jacket condition or electrical integrity.

For a functional motion claim, the actual documented joint movement must preserve slack without connector pullout, pinching or anchor slip. The joint limits, inspection views and acceptable strain/slip conditions must first be bound; no arbitrary load or “safe” distance is invented here. Do not substitute a hidden damage flag for ordinary observation.

The cheapest next technical checks, **if new evidence later un-parks N022**, are: (1) source-bound1:1 span/anchor/grasp geometry; (2) compatibility of actual bundle section with selected helix and ribbon opening; (3) a bounded opening/recovery/friction response and retained/wrong-wrap observation check. Reject an infeasible path before material simulation. No purchases or physical testing were conducted or authorized by this checkpoint.
