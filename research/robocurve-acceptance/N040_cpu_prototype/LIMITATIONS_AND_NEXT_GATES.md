## What the executed result means

The nominal parameter set permits tool-driven extraction, inward leg deformation and recovery after the oversized shoulder clears. Nearby contact, wrong surface, partial extraction, panel motion with a still-captured clip, plastic damage and absent initial retention all fail the success gate. A zero-retention setup is not rewarded for being already free. Release is audited from remaining oversized material and contact state, not a fixed extraction threshold. Tests check virtual-work derivatives, force balance and that falsifying the release audit cannot change the dynamics.

This is a reduced planar quasistatic model. Its extraction axis is a local panel-normal coordinate, not a measured door/world frame. Panel dead weight, gravity-driven sag, clip inertia and tangential motion are absent; a free axial coordinate can remain where numerical continuation leaves it. That persistence is not evidence of real released-panel stability. It cannot validate authentic leg topology, stiffness, head-carrier geometry, fracture/reuse, tool insertion access, 3D fork fit, impact load, multi-clip load sharing or an entire door. Some assumed shapes cause the reduced deformation mode to cross the axis; they are rejected as outside the model domain. Wrong-face displacement also exceeds the tool-overclosure domain. Neither result is a real-part failure prediction. Contact-step and penalty checks preserve the nominal release outcome, but peak force is not fully converged. No measured force is available for calibration.
