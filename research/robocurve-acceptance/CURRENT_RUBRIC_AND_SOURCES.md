## What the review agent actually sees

One message contains structured submission fields, setup screenshot, six sampled frames from each model/demo video, a code excerpt capped at40,000characters,15 text-similar internal reference tasks, recently rejected same-team tasks and10 similar external definitions. It does not receive team names or reviewers' comments. Humans make every decision.

Consequences: put the essential real-world equipment/procedure/outcome and measurement method in the structured task fields and concise README. A critical manual justification buried in a large archive may not be present in the excerpt. This is a presentation constraint, not permission to address/manipulate the reviewer. “Earlier feedback was not provided” cannot be disproved merely because comments exist on GitHub; the model input may not contain them.

## Current rules versus old outcomes

Earlier reviewer comments remain valid records of what happened. They do not supersede explicit Version21 permission for simulator-state experts, procedural instructions or proportionate minor feedback. Conversely, Version21 does not retroactively reverse our four final decisions. Their importance failures require authentic procedure/novelty evidence, not a controller-policy argument.

The strict source-to-outcome chain remains: exact operation → identifiable equipment → documented procedure → qualifying importance → core physical skill preserved → measurable outcome. An unresolved gate is conditional. Our engineering checks can be stricter for cost control, but must be labeled internal recommendations rather than official rubric requirements.
