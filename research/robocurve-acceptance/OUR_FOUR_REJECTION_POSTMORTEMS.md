## Code findings and missing evidence chains

**#1647:** task.json context claims generic gravitational moment adjustment and setting retention; fidelity identifies damped pivot, translating counterweight and free pin. scene.json supplies the authored assembly; scoring.py evaluates measured predicates/history and released hold; expert.py uses measured-frame feedback and14D robot commands. No inspected task definition identifies an actual manufacturer/model or a source proving adjustment is regular core work. Native probes establish a balanced fixture, not a recognizable occupational procedure. Simulator-state feedback is allowed under supplied Version21 and is not the problem.

**#1703:** task.json explicitly calls it a bench service fixture, not a particular remote, with an extended handle for YAM and uncalibrated2.5N/m cover return. scene.json goals collect two old cells, match correct new-cell polarity/contact states and close the cover. scoring.py includes one-to-one eligible replacement matching. These support a robust simulation of the authored exchange, not novelty against the named DexHand remote-battery task. Cover support and additional cell count cannot alone establish a different core ability. The occupational chain is also absent.

**#1733:** scene.json, build_assets.py and scoring.py implement the actual twelve spring groups, free plate and capture key, correct polarity/geometry and retained hold. This supports the claimed mechanics. task.json's context asserts general battery-pack closure without identifying a deployed twelve-AA cross-key assembly procedure. NOVELTY.md contrasts real neighboring preload tasks; that does not establish external commercial use. The final measurement design exists and is detailed but physically unvalidated; [appeal report](PR1733_APPEAL_EVIDENCE.md) separates its chronology from the final importance failure.

**#1772:** task.json explicitly disclaims a standard server, electrical/data connection and calibrated insertion forces. scene.json uses old/new storage and cooler identities, keyed seating, collection regions and passive lid closure. expert.py is a fixed robot-action replay. This is faithful to its own generic service puzzle; it does not preserve an identified OEM procedure. Calling missing interfaces outside scope cannot retain importance borrowed from those interfaces.

## Why engineering audits missed them

The submitted code has meaningful finite-state checks, measured scoring, reset logic, source-bound receipts and robot commands. Those audits answer implementation questions after an operation has been chosen. They do not test whether a named occupation actually performs that operation, whether the key equipment interface was deleted, or whether an earlier external task already measures the ability. Internal “review pass” labels must never stand in for the evidence chain. A single fatal importance or construct-validity failure cannot be voted away by successful execution checks.

## Version21 correction: do not add new imaginary rejection gates

Do not reject a scripted expert for reading object poses/joint/latch state; Version21 expressly allows that. Do not require every procedural instruction to omit steps; only the decision being tested must remain unprovided. Do not label two tasks duplicates because both involve preload transfer or hold/insert/release if objects, setting and success condition differ materially. Keep engineering and novelty objections specific. These corrections remove unsupported objections without reversing any of the four importance decisions.
