## N013: resolve the #1562 novelty risk

The controlling Version 21 test is the actual objects, manipulation and equivalent goal in the actual setting. Shared elementary turning/inserting motions alone are insufficient for a duplicate finding. Conversely, a different trade or different material does not by itself create a new ability.

**Accepted operation independently re-read:** [#1562](https://github.com/robocurve/benchmark-tasks/pull/1562), at the official main pin above, requires a 15 mm copper pipe seated in a fixed valve socket, a separate olive before the nut, actual spanner engagement/regrasp and tightening 3/4–1 1/4 turns beyond calibrated snug without overcompression. Its code models acquisition, nut thread progression, olive placement, spanner flats and retained completion. It does not verify live plumbing flow; novelty cannot be established simply by choosing a different physical proof test for our fitting.

**New corroborating drawing:** [BenchCAD LAPP drawing](https://github.com/BenchCAD-org/benchcad-2/blob/035f467142888edfd0bd7cd73906f9b155c8f43c/docs/assets/refs/metric_plastic_cable_gland_drawing.png), attributed there to LAPP DB53111000EN v17. We viewed the image, not just generated CAD code. For example, its ST-M M20×1.5 row identifies article 53111020, 25 mm body flats, 27.6 mm maximum cap diameter, 34–37 mm overall length and 6–13 mm cable clamping range. These are **mirror-drawing observations**, not independent authentication of that datasheet or the installation sheet. They show an axial threaded cap acting on the internal annular cable interface. The drawing does not verify cap torque, locknut choice, supplied seal state or assembly order.

| Ability / goal | Accepted #1562 | Current N013 proposal | Finding |
|---|---|---|---|
|Acquire and seat the retained member|Rigid pipe enters socket stop.|Flexible cable passes through gland.|Real material difference; no source-backed manipulation challenge established beyond feeding a member through a fitting.|
|Build the annular compression interface|Loose olive and nut must be correctly placed.|Factory gland seal and cap; actual supplied state unknown.|Changing the annular component is not a demonstrated new assembly ability. Do not invent loose-seal handling to increase novelty.|
|Engage thread and tighten with reaction|Nut, spanner flats and short strokes/regrasp; avoid under/overcompression.|Cap nut and body reaction, torque unknown.|Same substantive fitting-tightening capability, not merely the abstract verb “turn.”|
|Mount the fitting|Valve already fixed.|Potential body/panel/locknut installation.|An ordinary extra fastening stage is insufficient by itself. Two-sided access is a cost, not automatic novelty.|
|Obtain useful retained completion|Correct member seat, annular assembly and tightening range.|Cable strain relief and panel retention; seal claim still unverified.|Different service function, but proposed method still establishes a compressed member in a threaded fitting. A tug test changes observation, not necessarily skill.|

**Strongest case for retaining N013:** a real flexible jacket might require consequential torsion/slip management while body/panel reactions are controlled, with a strain-relief failure that is causally distinct from a pipe joint. Under Version 21 that could matter. However, neither the blocked installation sheet nor the accessible drawing establishes such a central extra capability in our proposed task. We cannot use a hypothetical future mechanism to clear the present design.

**Research resolution: REJECT the current N013 build.** This closes the “maybe a panel and different seal rescue it” path. The conclusion is an acceptance-risk decision supported by the detailed mechanical comparison, not a claim that reviewers have officially rejected cable glands. The #1562 overlap extends to the actual compression-fitting workflow, beyond elementary motions; the present distinction does not justify implementation expenditure.

[#17](https://github.com/robocurve/benchmark-tasks/pull/17) remains a different operation: its metadata fixes the gland and cable entry and scores a released 3–7 cm drip loop. It does not assemble the gland. #1411 wire-nut splicing and #1501 busbar torque are additional fastening neighbors, not automatic duplicates. Their existence does not strengthen N013's missing positive distinction from #1562.

**Physical scoring/access if someone revisits the concept:** torque alone cannot prove strain relief or an IP seal. Real panel seating plus independently measured cable slip under a justified benign load would be required. The mirrored M20 dimensions fit inside nominal jaw aperture, but flats, cap, panel edge, wrench sweep, cable reaction and contact friction determine actual access. No tool force, torque or retention experiment was performed. These observations do not reverse REJECT.

**Strongest hypothetical reviewer rejection — our reconstruction, not a quotation:** this is another threaded compression-fitting assembly, with the retained member and annular seal changed and an incidental panel fastener added; no distinct central capability is demonstrated. Any turn-count-to-retention shortcut would further detach success from the claimed cable strain relief.

## Replacement finalist 1: N041 — full-size Type A wiper replacement

**Decision: HOLD.** This is a narrower evidence-backed research choice, not permission to revive the miniaturized #1335 implementation. The bank's earlier internal REJECT was based on the matching operation; the newly inspected public final decision/status and exact equipment procedure warrant HOLD rather than automatic rejection or novelty PASS.

**Exact real equipment/procedure read.** [Pinned 2013 U.S. Hyundai Elantra owner's manual](https://github.com/Kmberry1989/Elantra/blob/0f7b23efdcf6ecc48919fb781be7e95a1b8f0f06/2013%20Hyundai%20Elantra%20%28UD%29%20Owners%20Manual.pdf), printed 7-30/7-31, PDF pages 321/322. We extracted the text and viewed page 7-31. Type A shows the real plastic locking clip and curved-hook connection: raise/rotate to expose it, compress it, slide the blade down and lift off; installation reverses removal. Type B is separate and must not be silently substituted. The manual also warns against allowing the arm to fall onto the windshield. The foreword includes U.S. regulatory context; the mirror identifies the U.S. 2013 manual. **Hyundai-authored procedure verified through this copy; original Hyundai-hosted bytes not independently matched.** Actual vehicle VIN/arm/blade part numbers, dimensions and loads are still unbound.

**Occupation evidence.** Mirrored national employment for 49-3023 is **704,640**. O*NET 30.2 task 23533 classifies routine/scheduled vehicle maintenance as Core; 23534 includes maintenance checklists. An older task mirror explicitly calls accessory work including windshield wipers Core under 49-3023.01 (task 2974). Crucially, **that explicit wiper statement is not present in the newer consolidated 30.2 rows**, so it is historical corroboration, not a freshly verified current exact-duty claim. The actual maintenance manual supplies the wear-triggered replacement procedure. Together this is stronger than the Euro-cylinder case, but it is not a measured frequency estimate for every mechanic. The jobs argument remains bounded/conditional.

**Current novelty and comparator.** [#1335](https://github.com/robocurve/benchmark-tasks/pull/1335), head `ae21c542313e35be184626ffe24f3354dc166e0a`, tests the same wiper swap. Its final review rejected a 54 cm-wide windshield, 24 cm blade and unestablished 0.2 Nm arm loading. Its metadata also admits a switched slide/catch with no latch tab to press. These are source facts, not evidence that such shortcuts are acceptable.

The public page is final/closed; its final event is November 12 at approximately **3:53 a.m. EST**, over 48 hours before this decision. That is different from an accepted task or an active earlier revision hold. It does not prove current private-board clearance or erase the identical-operation comparison. Under Version 21, do not call the same operation novel solely because it is enlarged; determine whether the prior task is an eligible current reference/claim. No current accepted full-scale counterpart was found by the targeted public queries; absence is not exhaustive clearance.

**Construct validity and physical scoring.** Preserve the actual full-size vehicle cowl, windshield, arm, spring/pivot and blade/adapter around the working region. A representative fixture may reproduce those real surroundings; a small tabletop wiper does not. Do not force continuous bimanual holding if the real arm remains stable when raised: the manual's fall warning is not proof of an unstable hinge or a measured torque curve. Retain the actual clip release/capture rather than snapping the blade into a distance-triggered joint.

A proposed score can use the actual adapter's seated/captured condition, independent benign release-direction retention check, correct released blade orientation and normal returned arm/glass contact. A correctly removed old blade is genuine partial progress; full score requires retained new-blade engagement, not a hook-proximity flag. Damage/broken latch invalidates progress. Do not require arbitrary robot return-home or a particular scrap-tray placement. No wiping-performance, spring-torque or damage-speed threshold is asserted from this manual; define and rehearse numeric tolerances/test load on the selected real equipment before a scorer is specified.

**YAM access and realistic cost.** High/uncertain full-scale reach burden; medium local clip/hook modeling burden. Check approach from the ordinary service side with the entire palm/wrist, not just the fingertips at the adapter. One arm may need to support the actual arm while the other actuates the small tab and performs the hook slide; whether it does must be measured, not designed into a trainer. The full blade's mass, sweep and collision with cowl/glass remain. No actual-scale reach, grip-slip, clip-force or spring curve was measured. This candidate is not necessarily cheaper than N013; it has a clearer source-backed procedure and a concrete test that can kill it before coding.

**Earliest inexpensive kill test:** bind an available real Type A assembly; measure the arm/blade/cowl envelope and all necessary poses at full scale; overlay the stock gripper/palm frames; measure clip actuation and arm behavior. Stop if access needs deleted cowl/glass, resized parts, weakened spring or an enlarged tab. Then check actual release/re-engagement and retention by hand on the genuine mechanism, if available. None was performed here.

**Strongest hypothetical reviewer rejection:** the task merely recreates earlier #1335, with vehicle reach or force again reduced, and the real plastic clip replaced by a magic hook latch. If authentic scale is kept, the necessary access may be outside the demonstrated YAM workspace. Historical occupation language plus generic maintenance counts may also be insufficient to prove the exact regular-duty claim. These are live HOLD reasons, not generic caveats.

## Replacement finalist 2: N037 — actual brake-pad retainers in a carrier

**Decision: HOLD.** Choose this over the unbound micrometer/gait-belt/tape-dispenser alternatives because an exact matching equipment procedure was actually read, and brake repair is explicitly Core in the 30.2 snapshot. It still has the more serious immediate ownership/importance question of the two replacements.

**Exact real equipment/procedure read.** [2013 Elantra L4-1.8L front-disc repair procedure](https://github.com/Kmberry1989/Elantra/blob/0f7b23efdcf6ecc48919fb781be7e95a1b8f0f06/2013%20Hyundai%20Elantra%20L4-1.8L/pages/588.html) in the supplied CHARM archive. Under replacement, the real procedure pivots the caliper after loosening the guide-rod bolt and replaces shims, retainers and pads in the carrier. We viewed its [diagram](https://github.com/Kmberry1989/Elantra/blob/0f7b23efdcf6ecc48919fb781be7e95a1b8f0f06/2013%20Hyundai%20Elantra%20L4-1.8L/images/IMP07Q313/asian244/816884588.jpeg): A carrier, B pads, C retainers, D shims. Retainers are real separate interfaces, not invented colored alignment keys.

**Provenance limit:** the repository's README identifies the workshop download from charm.li; its original URL was attempted and blocked. We verified this specific reproduced procedure and diagram, **not the identity/revision of an original Hyundai service bulletin or a current replacement kit**. No Carlson/OEM retainer part number, exact clip geometry, force or reusable-condition rule was verified. Do not call it an authenticated manufacturer manual. The same mirror's owner manual cannot authenticate the separate workshop archive.

**Occupation evidence.** 49-3023 has mirrored May 2025 employment **704,640**. O*NET 30.2 task **23525, Core**, says repair, reline, replace and adjust brakes (incumbent source 08/2022). The exact workshop operation places retainer handling inside ordinary pad replacement. This provides a materially tighter procedure-to-core-duty link than Euro cylinders and generic door repair. It does not establish that isolated clip installation is independently important enough, or the frequency of a particular kit across all mechanics.

**Current novelty/ownership.** Fresh abutment and pad-retainer queries returned no direct matches; the broader brake query found the important neighbors. [#1967](https://github.com/robocurve/benchmark-tasks/pull/1967), head `d022b544fe59d403a8dbfd06d142fc86253e2ac3`, fits rear pads after piston wind-back. Its source fixes caliper/carrier together, omits the rotor and starts pads in extended channels. `pad_seated` checks pose on ledges; it does not manipulate separate retainer clips. The new review explicitly requires a rotor-mounted carrier, separate caliper and genuine wind-back/reassembly. That pending revision could encompass more genuine pad hardware than its current code does.

Its review was November 14 at approximately **1:26 a.m. EST**, with an explicit 48-hour revision opportunity. Thus this is a potentially active claim, not a lapsed final rejection to ignore. [#1089](https://github.com/robocurve/benchmark-tasks/pull/1089) is final/closed after using a rectangular rotor and flat hinged bridge; [#976](https://github.com/robocurve/benchmark-tasks/pull/976) is another surfaced caliper-over-disc neighbor whose current scope/status remains to inspect. **No ownership clearance.**

Under Version 21, actual spring-retainer seating around real carrier lands could differ from piston wind-back and pad/caliper assembly, even though both concern brakes. Conversely, removing most of a pad replacement and renaming the leftover clip is not automatically a distinct ability. The burden is to demonstrate meaningful retainer-specific alignment, spring capture and subsequent pad freedom. If the earlier legitimate scope already covers this, or the only remaining action is ordinary clip placement, REJECT rather than submit a narrowed clone. Do not claim a defective earlier implementation forfeits its active hold.

**Construct validity and physical scoring.** Use the real matched carrier, rotor, separate normally supported caliper and the actual retainers/shims/pads required by the selected procedure. An honestly bounded start may have the wheel/caliper already moved by normal service; it cannot fuse them or substitute long empty rails. Do not claim a complete brake service if piston work, reassembly or bleeding is outside scope. Preserve the retainer's actual spring tabs, lands, orientation and pad-contact surfaces.

Proposed physical observations: both correct retainers fully seated and stable after release; actual matched pad ears engage the retained surfaces with source-compatible freedom and no folded tab; no displaced shim, reversed retainer or rotor interference. Use inspection photos and an independent benign pad insertion/travel/retention rehearsal. Pose of a floating clip alone is inadequate. Full credit cannot come from an empty carrier with a hidden “installed” latch. Exact tolerances, forces and partial-credit thresholds remain unmeasured; this is a scoring design, not a verified scorer or vehicle brake-safety certification.

**YAM access and realistic cost.** Medium/high, not rigid-only: thin spring steel, grip orientation, preload, local interference and holding reaction dominate. The OEM-style drawing provides no numeric clearance proof. Stock fingers may cover the very edges needed to press a retainer into the carrier. A normal small tool may be allowed if the actual procedure supports it; no large fake handle, giant clip or hand-free magnetic fixture. Real rotor/caliper surroundings must remain in every intermediate pose. No clip force, grip slip or access path was measured.

**Earliest inexpensive kill test:** first resolve #1967/#976 ownership and compare real clip engagement against their legitimate scope. If it survives, identify a VIN-compatible actual retainer/carrier set, map stock-finger/tool access 1:1, and measure genuine seating/release plus an inverted/folded/partly seated negative control. Stop before scene work if a full finger cannot engage the necessary land, the spring cannot be represented causally, or inspection/functional pad travel fails to distinguish bad clips. No purchase or experiment occurred.

**Strongest hypothetical reviewer rejection:** this is an ordinary substep of an earlier team's still-reserved brake-pad task, with the defining vehicle/rotor/caliper constraints removed to make clip placement easy. Even a real clip model may be too minor alone to inherit the occupation's importance. A bounding-box score could reward a folded or unretained clip. This rejection is concrete enough to keep N037 on HOLD despite the stronger occupation evidence.

## YAM evidence: what was and was not established

Continue to use the previously pinned public adapter `0059d02a1a91bc6834c1d0846022891df0c78730`, not whatever its repository's HEAD later becomes. The adapter is evidence for one simulated embodiment, not confirmation of the live evaluation revision or a hardware force rating.

- Nominal maximum aperture is 93.9 mm; configured gripper effort is 50 N and wrist effort 7 Nm. Two finger joint entries do not establish a 100 N pinch. Joint torque does not equal transferable clip force or torque at a part.
- New static calculation: transform each visual STL by its URDF visual origin and closed finger-joint origin, then express it relative to TCP `(0,0,0.134)` m. Each complete finger/carriage visual mesh spans about **71.57 × 68.00 × 93.84 mm** in those frame axes; distal visual geometry reaches about **12.84 mm beyond TCP**. The two meshes have intentionally overlapping carriage bounds. These figures are **not fingertip-pad thickness, a rectangular obstacle to impose everywhere, or final imported collision hulls**.
- The calculation explicitly includes the offsets ignored by a raw STL bounding-box measurement. It still cannot clear a small wiper tab or retainer recess: local pad geometry, full palm/wrist, actual opening and the whole swept/regrasp path must be checked against selected real parts. No arm IK, collision simulation, actuation or task scene was run.

[Result and assumptions](evidence/final-two-gate/stock-finger-static-envelope.json); [standalone source-analysis script](evidence/final-two-gate/measure_pinned_finger_envelope.py). The script reads existing pinned CAD and performs transforms only. These are additional static access bounds, not robot feasibility demonstrations.

## Final spending decision and next non-implementation gates

**No task is GO.** N013 and N006 are retired in their present forms. N041 and N037 replace them as the two bounded evidence investigations, not as approved implementations. The next useful work is source/ownership resolution and genuine-hardware access/measurement on available equipment, if separately pursued; a manual existing is insufficient.

For either, inability to distinguish a genuinely retained endpoint from a wrong/loose assembly ends the proposed scoring model. Do not move to native testing to discover these failures.

Original LAPP, ECHO, occupation and newly attempted manufacturer/distributor/manual-library sites remain blocked. Their exact URLs and errors remain preserved. None is described as read. The new mirror evidence changes what we know and the build recommendation; it does not justify fabricating the still-missing product dimensions, force curves, current ownership or successful robot trials.
