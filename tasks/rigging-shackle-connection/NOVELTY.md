# Occupational grounding and comparison

The occupation is rigger (O*NET 49-9096.00). The concrete operation is connecting already selected, inspected lifting hardware: align a bow shackle around a lug, insert the matching screw pin through both eyes and the lug, and seat its collar fully. It is common repeated rigging assembly, not generic bench balancing or an invented equipment service story. Load selection, inspection and proof lifting are outside the isolated manipulation objective.

The reference class is Green Pin G-4161, nominal GPGHBB16 (3.25 t catalogue size). The manufacturer describes screw-pin hardware specifically for frequent rigging assembly/disassembly. The original simulation assets use published envelope dimensions and 3/4-10 UNC pitch. No manufacturer's CAD, texture, certification logo or original drawing is embedded. The catalogue rating identifies the reference size; simulation does not inherit that rating.

## Essential interfaces retained

- A free bow body with an unthreaded near eye and threaded far eye.
- A matching through-pin that must cross the near eye, lug bore and far eye, then engage a pitch-coupled contact thread.
- A collar stop and visible full-depth outcome. Free spinning or a pin merely placed at the finish does not satisfy turn history.
- A closed sling link already threaded through the bow. The operation begins after that preparation step; no credit is awarded for it.

The passive alignment nest and supported link are authored unloaded bench aids. They are not claimed manufacturer fixtures. The left hand aligns the bow, then parks; simultaneous crowded-hand contact is deliberately avoided at this real hardware scale. This is a bimanual sequence, not a claimed continuous two-hand preload transfer. Square-profile contact flanks approximate UNC thread pitch/engagement; stress, certified torque and lifting capacity are excluded.
