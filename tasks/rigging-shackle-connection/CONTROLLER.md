# Executable expert and limits

`expert.py` binds the official Inspect policy to `feedback.Controller`. The only live inputs are the SDK's 14 robot joint/gripper values and time. No native object dictionary, scorer state, simulator handle or pose-write API is imported by the policy. `controller.json` contains authored targets and separately labelled nominal body poses used only by offline audits.

The left arm approaches and grips the outer bow, translates it 6 mm into alignment, releases into the passive nest, and parks clear. The right arm grips the top of the matching pin tab, lifts it vertically from its cradle, transfers above the eyes, and inserts through the near eye and lug. It then executes 48 measured-joint 45-degree strokes, with 20 mm clearance, wrist unwind and regrasp between them. The ideal pin descent is 2.54 mm per turn. That schedule commands actions; only real thread contacts can produce actual advancement. A jam is a failure, not a reason to write the expected pin pose.

Translation and rotation use a shared interpolation fraction, preserving screw lead between endpoints rather than only at targets. Open-jaw return uses 25 mm/s; threading speed/turns and final scoring are unchanged. The 20 mm clearance leaves a nominal 4.15 mm head/finger margin before FK error and contact tolerances.

`Motion.target` uses the pinned URDF damped-least-squares IK with rate-limited joint references. Each phase waits for measured TCP position and orientation, a plausible nonempty jaw aperture while closed, and its dwell interval. Joint tracking is bounded; no progress for eight seconds, a phase timeout, stale/nonfinite observation or action-limit overrun fails explicitly. Grip/regrasp acquisition can reopen and retry at most twice; other motion stalls fail safely. Expected part widths, including the pin tab projected onto the jaw axis, determine the measured aperture band. A phase ending is not a full-score demonstration. The policy requests stop after the complete plan; the independent native scorer/validator must still prove the full outcome.

The final pin release opens partially first, lifts clear, then fully opens while withdrawing. The bow support is not maintained simultaneously with threading: the real-sized hardware otherwise crowded the grippers, which offline collision checks detected. The nest is an explicit passive bench aid; there is no dynamic weld, grasp attachment or motorized task joint.

The nominal IK audit solves all authored endpoints. The sequence audit runs the real controller under ideal joint tracking and synthetic solid-part jaw apertures. That fixture can catch unreachable paths, branch failures and time overruns but assumes successful contact. The robot audit samples critical poses using actual pinned URDF convex pieces. None proves a physical grasp, contact-force capacity, actuator tracking or continuous collision avoidance. The native control prefixes and whole episodes remain required.

The repaired source completes ideal tracking in 7,217 actions (360.85 s), compared with 13,191 (659.55 s) on the preserved historical source. The 18,000-action limit remains generous for model retries. All 299 endpoint and 639 full-plan actual-FK samples passed in the local repair checks. Independent clean-source reproduction and native contact validation remain separate requirements.
