# Occupational grounding

Heavy and tractor-trailer truck drivers, SOC53-3032, perform cargo securement as a core task. O*NET states: “Secure cargo for transport, using ropes, blocks, chain, binders, or covers.” The BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook independently lists “Secure cargo for transport, using ropes, blocks, chains, or covers” among duties and reports: “Heavy and tractor-trailer truck drivers held about 2.2 million jobs in 2025.”

Sources: https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/53-3032.00 and https://www.bls.gov/ooh/transportation-and-material-moving/heavy-and-tractor-trailer-truck-drivers.htm . Source retrieval hashes and exact excerpts are in occupation-evidence.json. The employment count is for one occupation, not overlapping categories, and exceeds500,000. It does not establish that every driver uses this product or that all2.2million use cargo bars daily.

US Cargo Control's commercial round-tube load bar is explicitly for trucks/enclosed trailers and describes spreading pressure from its handle and nonslip pads. Harbor Freight96811 provides an actual cargo-bar operating/release procedure. The benchmark isolates removal of that equipment class as part of unloading/handling restraints. It does not claim to simulate driving, cargo loading, regulatory certification or the whole occupation.

Manufacturer reference: https://manuals.harborfreight.com/manuals/96000-96999/96811-792363968119.pdf pp2–3. Commercial equipment evidence: https://www.uscargocontrol.com/products/round-tube-cargo-load-bars-w-2x4-pads-4-pack.json . Full-scale span and contact/load path distinguish this from a practice-only miniature trainer. Unknown internal dimensions are explicitly original authored geometry, not a claim of manufacturer CAD or measurements.

A final importance review must assess whether this bounded removal operation remains meaningfully representative of core cargo restraint work. A large occupational count by itself cannot rescue an unrelated custom mechanism.
