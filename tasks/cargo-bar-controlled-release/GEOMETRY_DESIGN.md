# Bounded operation and original mechanical design

The operation is **release and controlled shortening of a preclamped cargo load bar**. Harbor Freight96811 manual p2 states: “To release the load, pull back on the Latch Lock and raise the Handle to its full forward position.” Extension instructions identify pulling the tube after opening the latch/handle, while p3 lists rack, gear set, two pawls, springs, release lever, tubes and feet. This establishes equipment and procedure, not hidden dimensions or an exact cam design.

The left robot grips the loaded inner tube; the right squeezes the independent trigger out of its keeper and rotates the handle fully forward. Two original physical cams lift the independently hinged pawls clear. The robot slides the inner tube back while controlling the outer tube through its held handle. The rack backdrives the pinion through contact. The right hand centers the shortened bar, lowers it onto two cradles, releases and withdraws. No stroke counter, gear equality, attachment or controller state write advances a component.

The 1.2m prepared span is inside the manufacturer's40–70in class. The bar is rotated about its long axis so the handle sweeps horizontally at stock YAM working height; this is an authored orientation. The frame substitutes for vehicle walls; cradles support the finished bar. Initial clamp force comes from feet, without a hidden anchor or cradle support. There is no cargo, vehicle rating or transport certification.

All internal geometry is original: convex involute pinion/rack flanks,24-tooth ratchet, two bowed pawls, physical cam sectors/followers, open handle hub, sliding trigger/keeper and spring foot cartridges. The service-window housing exposes the interfaces. Only directly connected joint bodies receive adjacency filtering. Rack/pinion, pawl/wheel, trigger/keeper and cam/follower contacts remain enabled. There are no broad contact exclusions.

scene.json contains every convex face/vertex, primitive, mass/inertia, material and joint frame. PARAMETERS.md and parameter-provenance.json distinguish authored and derived values from source dimensions. No hardware measurements exist. The pad envelope is from a separate US Cargo Control product. The cam/pawl design realizes the documented parts and function; it is not a manufacturer's hidden assembly drawing.

Engineering failures: sliced-box teeth jammed and became convex flanks; a flat pawl arm clipped adjacent teeth and became a bowed link; sliced cams jammed at internal faces and became convex sectors; coupled robot holds exposed circular-path drift, repaired by exact circle IK. An unloaded handle relaxes, so final scoring does not require an arbitrary retained angle. Release remains mechanically necessary to retract without forcing or skipping teeth.

Remaining risks include grip friction/trigger purchase, release under preload, passive handle relaxation and solver differences. Exact-source Isaac robot runs must establish feasibility. Ideal tracking, test-rig actuation and initialized diagnostic final poses are not demonstrations.
