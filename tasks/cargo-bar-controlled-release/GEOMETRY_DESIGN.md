# Work in progress: original cargo-bar fixture

This branch is under implementation and is not offline-ready or submission-ready. No source/evidence from an earlier task qualifies this mechanism.

Harbor Freight item96811 manufacturer manual p2 specifies40–70 inch length, pulling the latch lock and raising the handle to extend the tube, slowly ratcheting to secure it, then closing the handle flat. P3 identifies inner/outer tubes, rack, gear set, two pawls, pawl spring, release-lever spring, release lever and handle. It does not provide tooth profiles, force ratings, spring constants or those component dimensions. All such fixture parameters here are original benchmark-authored approximations. The source manual SHA256 is a486b18f2445e9a74fd1230a538f6bbf602ac46e1e3eb6f7d9c165c0ecf14489.

US Cargo Control's primary commercial-bar product describes truck/enclosed-trailer load locking, padded feet, handle-generated spreading force,89–103.94 inch range, and expressly permits shortening the non-tension-lock end for smaller vehicles. This is separate equipment evidence, not a claim that its dimensions belong to item96811. Its retrieved product JSON SHA256 is fe11382d0fa409e6c85e7a7b7532f7af771df8a4fa991b68d8b0e77dd314a96b. The benchmark's1.2 m test span lies inside the documented40–70 inch item96811 range; the mechanism itself is not miniaturized.

The proposed bounded operation starts with an unloaded bar supported at working height, too short to clamp. The robot releases the latch, opens the handle fully to free the rack, extends the tube while stabilizing the outer body, ratchets to compress both end pads against the test walls, then closes/latches the operating handle and lets go. Pressure is a fixture design quantity, not manufacturer data or a cargo certification. Final physical retention, foot contacts, latch state and numeric clearances must determine score; no virtual per-stroke rule may advance the rack.

Mechanism design: original contact-driven involute rack/pinion, coaxial ratchet wheel, separate driving and holding pawls on passive joints, physical release cam/latch, telescoping guide, compliant end pads and fixed wall frame. Gear/pawl/rack contact remains enabled. Only directly joined bearing/guide bodies use the normal joint adjacency filter. The round bar is turned about its length so the handle sweeps horizontally, preserving stock-scale equipment while keeping the robot tool upright. The prepared fixture supports weight, not clamp force.

O*NET heavy/tractor-trailer truck drivers explicitly secure cargo using restraints; BLS records about2.2 million such workers. Verify exact core-task quote/count and cite primary sources before the final importance decision. Do not imply every driver uses this particular product.

Closest known pending comparison is PR1285 (ratchet a prewrapped strap around a crate and latch the handle before a jolt). Its published task uses a virtual per-stroke winding rule. Internal implementation differences alone do not establish novelty. This candidate must retain and test actual free telescope release/extension, opposing wall/foot alignment and a rigid compression load path, as distinct from flexible strap winding/tension. Generic ratchet mechanics and final handle latching are shared. This is a material risk to resolve before counting the task. No direct cargo-bar PR match was found in the scoped public search; full board coverage remains restricted.

Implemented core: original convex involute pinion teeth and trapezoidal rack teeth replace the initially jammed box-slice prototype.41 flank samples have zero inferred overlap. Actual MuJoCo CPU torque drive moves the rack about19.6 mm in0.25 s with0.5 friction; lead error stays below0.1 mm, without a gear equality or position actuator. Disabled-contact negative control leaves the rack stationary, and friction0.08/0.25 variants also pass. Three executable gearing tests pass. These are isolated CPU mechanism probes, not a complete robot task or Isaac evidence.

The current original USD scene has one wall frame and a free-root cargo bar with two tubes, two spring pads, gear set, handle, trigger and two pawls on eight passive joints. Convex mesh collision authoring and conservative mesh SAT were added to the reusable helpers. Initial allowed-contact geometry reports zero overlap, and every body, including the1.32 m frame, fits the calibrated stock top-camera view. The round tube/handle assembly is rotated about its own length so the handle sweeps horizontally; no object scale or robot capability is changed.

Pad springs are authored12 kN/m, with4 mm travel and2 N s/m damping, not manufacturer rubber measurements. The bar's1.308 kg authored total mass needs about12.8 N vertical support. Two pads compressed1.667 mm each produce about20 N normal force each; with the modeled0.5 dynamic friction, their combined friction bound exceeds bar weight. This is a fixture design calculation, not a cargo rating. Planned coarse travel13.333 mm plus three15-degree ratchet teeth gives23.333 mm total extension and3.333 mm combined pad compression after taking up the20 mm initial end gaps; actual pawl backlash/contact must be measured in simulation, never assumed to succeed.
