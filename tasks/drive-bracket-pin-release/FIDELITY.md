# Procedure, hardware and fidelity

The manufacturer procedure is Dell OptiPlex 7060 Tower, *Removing the 2.5-inch drive from the drive bracket*, pp23–24. The preceding assembly removal disconnects SATA data/power and removes the drive assembly. The next step pulls one side of the bracket to disengage its pins from drive-side slots and lifts the drive out. The figure shows one-sided opening followed by tilting/lifting clear. This benchmark isolates that removed-assembly step. It does not claim chassis access, electrical connection, full computer repair, reinstalling software or functional drive diagnosis.

A prepared surrogate drive is retained by two opposed pin pairs and real open side bores. One side rail moves outward against a passive return force, physically withdrawing its pair of pins. The other pair remains until the drive's released edge is tilted up and the drive is translated away. A fixed bench nest supports the bracket, replacing a technician's stabilizing hand. The rail includes an enlarged handling extension so the supporting robot hand stays away from the drive grip. The drive is staged and the released rail returns before final success. No drive joint, snap, adhesion, software attachment or success-triggered pose correction exists.

Samsung's 870 EVO datasheet supplies only the external 100×69.85×6.8 mm envelope used here. It does not dimension this Dell bracket or establish mounting-hole interchangeability. All four 4.8 mm authored bores, 3 mm-diameter pins, 60 mm pin spacing, 0.9 mm radial bore clearance, handling extension, bracket shape and support geometry are benchmark-authored. The coarse bores permit the prescribed tilt without modeling rubber grommet deformation. They make alignment easier than some production brackets; real tool-less bracket compatibility is not asserted. There are no independently measured values.

The blue rail's plastic compliance is a lumped, spring-return prismatic joint (180 N/m, 1 N·s/m, 0–7 mm). Those values are **not Dell force data**. It approximates the outward release mode, not a calibrated bending beam, fatigue, yielding or distributed plastic strain. Its rigid rail can be reproduced using a linear guide and calibrated spring, while a true plastic flexure would require measured force/displacement qualification. Only that joint's adjacent parent/child collision is disabled to represent their continuous flexure. Every drive/pin/bore collision stays enabled.

The drive collision envelope represents a hollow case/PCB mass surrogate, not a solid block of aluminium. Its 45 g mass is authored, not a measured Samsung mass. Anchored base/supports use aluminium/polymer assumptions; the rail uses rigid polymer and steel pin approximations. Authored mass/COM/bounding-box inertia and uniform friction require native validation. The 480 Hz solver and 30 µm contact offset are benchmark numerical settings, not material measurements.

The task tests maintaining a release displacement while acquiring a wide, thin drive, disengaging opposite retention interfaces in the proper spatial order, avoiding the remaining pins, and leaving the drive supported. It does not test a cam handle, simple drop-in tray, generic lid hold or electrical connector seating. If real contacts or grasping fail, revise and revalidate rather than calling ideal tracking an expert demonstration.
