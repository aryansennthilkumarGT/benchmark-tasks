# Procedure fidelity and limitations

This is an original benchmark service fixture, not a dimensionally compatible DeWalt DCN680 or a measured tool digital twin. The manufacturer manual N496636, printed p12, FigJ, explicitly instructs lifting the jam-clear latch, opening the nosepiece door, removing a bent nail, closing the door, engaging the latch pin under both nosepiece arms, and pushing the latch until it locks. Those interfaces and the independent free fastener are represented physically. SOURCES.json records the genuine source hashes and scoped operation.

The battery is already absent, trigger locked, magazine empty and driver retracted. The task stops after steps3/4/6. It does not model firing, battery handling, unloading, driver reset, loaded operation or the optional use of pliers. The firing axis is +X, away from the robot bases. The fixture rests on its side with service access toward−Y; gravity is consequently in the service frame's+Z direction. It is visibly clamped to an original bench stand. Workholding is a disclosed preparation, not an alleged manufacturer accessory.

The essential constraints are physical: the bent head is wider than the closed muzzle; opening the actual hinged door creates an extraction path. The draw bail has its own hinge and an axially compliant end; a real crosspin must enter the mouths of both fixed arms. Closing the independent lever takes an eccentric past center and puts the compliant draw member in tension. The door, lever, bail and pin are separate colliding rigid bodies with all four parent-child contacts enabled. Neither an angle threshold nor a controller phase switches collision, creates a latch, attaches the brad or grants score.

FigJ establishes the external topology and sequence. It does **not** establish the hidden eccentric dimensions, elastic force law, masses, friction or the exposed pin length used here. All numerical fixture dimensions and physical parameters are benchmark-authored or explicitly derived; none are presented as OEM measurements. The52mm pin and5mm rear door overhang provide stock-gripper access. They change access relative to an unmeasured real product. This task tests the same service sequence and two-arm capture constraint, but makes no claim of matching OEM dexterity difficulty or force fidelity. The spring is an effective axial surrogate for wire compliance, not a measured bail constitutive model. The pre-bent brad is rigid; no plastic deformation, fracture, tool wear or residual stored firing energy is modeled.

Compound-density COM and principal inertias use explicit primitive integrals normalized to authored target masses. Overlapping solid components are counted twice in these lumped moments; they are not exact boolean-CAD volumes or measured material densities. The5µm initialized pin-contact deflection is an explicit numerical elastic-contact preparation, not an interference-fit manufacturing instruction. Native solver compatibility still requires testing.

The independent scorer requires geometric removal of every brad collider, complete placement in the tray, door seating, capture of both pin ends, positive draw extension and the actual past-center length derivative. Invalid body/joint relationships, excessive penetration, fixed-fixture movement or fastener loss latch zero. Full score requires a continuous2s witness-drift window and20mm robot clearance. Alternative intact physical strategies remain valid; the expert's sequence is not prescribed by scoring.

Supplemental MuJoCo probes apply capped, declared diagnostic forces/torques without robot grasps. The force-guided full cycle is not a native YAM demonstration. Ideal tracking assumes rigid grasps and brad settling; it cannot validate contact forces, friction, grasp acquisition, controller tracking, actuator loads or real hardware. No native Isaac, Dream-1 or physical hardware success is claimed. Validation remains pending until those experiments actually execute.
